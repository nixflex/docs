Voice support and automatic language behaviour depend on the selected route. A single worldwide language count does not describe every voice or channel.

## Choosing a language

Use the language control and current supported values for the agent's selected voice. Keep instructions in clear language and state whether the agent may switch when a caller asks. A prompt asking for a language cannot add support that the selected speech route lacks.

## Names and unusual words

Chosen recognition words help with names, services and unusual terms. New manual edits allow up to 20 entries, 50 Unicode characters each and a combined 450-byte budget including per-entry overhead. The byte limit can be reached sooner with some scripts. Keep phrases specific rather than copying whole instructions.

Live knowledge can contribute automatic recognition hints while manual choices take priority. Hints improve the chance of recognition; they do not force a transcript or control pronunciation. Unchanged older saved lists can be retained under compatibility rules, but edited lists must meet current limits.

## Speech and written channels differ

SMS and website chat do not use speech recognition. They have their own instructions and can use agent knowledge when their channel is enabled. A successful written answer does not validate a telephone accent, voice or interruption setting.

Test names, dates, prices, email addresses and a caller changing language. Confirm uncertain facts aloud. Use the actual intended time zone and country rather than a country-specific default copied from an example.
