## Write the brief

Describe who will use the app, what they need to do and which result proves success. For example: “Our team reviews missed calls, opens the confirmed booking result and assigns follow-up.” Keep the first version to one complete task.

List the screens, team roles and actions that need a confirmation. Say which calls or messages may be sent, who authorises them and what the app should show if the result is uncertain.

## Decide the customer boundary

Separate agents can have different instructions, but an organisation shares its team, balance and resources. For a SaaS serving unrelated businesses, ask the developer to explain how each customer is mapped to its own permitted account and data. A different agent name alone does not provide privacy between customers.

## Review before testing

| Review | Ask for evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Secrets | Keys stay on a server and never appear in the browser or AI prompt. |
| Permissions | Each person can access only the account and actions their role permits. |
| Results | Queued, completed, failed and unknown are shown separately. |
| Changes | Calls, texts, bookings and deletions have a clear review step. |

Test with invented records first. Agree on a small controlled live test and check the actual destination. A generated “success” screen is not proof that a message arrived or an appointment exists.
