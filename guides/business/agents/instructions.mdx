## Start with one job

Explain the business, who usually calls, what the agent should accomplish and what it must hand to a person. Give the greeting and tone in plain words. Avoid contradictory instructions or a long list of commands with no priority.

```text Example instructions
You answer enquiries for Willow Reception.
Use the reviewed business facts and ask one question at a time.
Explain our services and help the caller choose their next step.
If a fact is missing, say you need the team to confirm it.
Do not approve refunds or promise a callback unless our real process supports it.
Offer a transfer when the caller needs a person and that action is available.
```

Replace the example with your own checked facts. Do not paste customer records, credentials or private connection details into instructions.

## Separate rules from reference facts

| Keep in instructions | Put in knowledge |
| --- | --- |
| The agent’s role, tone and handoff limits | Detailed policies and service descriptions |
| Main opening hours, address and frequently asked prices | Long menus, catalogues and uncommon questions |
| When to ask a follow-up question | Facts that need source review and regular updates |

Small knowledge can be included with the agent’s instructions automatically; larger knowledge is searched for relevant facts. Review the live topics and test unknown answers rather than assuming every upload is available immediately.

## Test and refine

Save one change at a time and test a normal question, a correction and a question outside the business’s scope. Check the actual call result. A prompt that says “book” or “send a text” still needs the corresponding connection and enabled action.
