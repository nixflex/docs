title: "Conversation controls: balance turn-taking and action rules"
description: "Understand Nixflex turn-taking, silence handling and action controls, and why clear instructions and real-call tests matter alongside enabled feature switches."
---

Conversation controls shape when an agent speaks, waits, acts or ends a call. They combine saved settings, action permissions and the caller's actual speech. A sentence in a prompt cannot guarantee a particular timing or enable a disabled action.

## Instructions and facts have different jobs

Keep the main call instructions focused on identity, purpose, essential facts, questions, action rules and fallback behaviour. Put longer details in agent knowledge. SMS and website instructions are separate; outbound calls add a specific purpose.

## Waiting and ending

Silence handling, an explicit wait action and the maximum call duration serve different purposes. A wait action is useful when a caller asks for time; it does not remove the hard duration limit. Phone-menu navigation can wait for a menu while listening for a human, subject to its configured limits.

An end-call request can politely finish a completed task. The `func_end_call:false` setting disables the configured silence and maximum-duration timers; it is not a general prohibition on model-requested hangups. A transfer request is not proof that another person answered.

## Interruption and uncertainty

Short turns and one question at a time make interruptions easier to handle. Check names, dates and important numbers instead of guessing from a weak transcript. No setting guarantees perfect interruption detection in noisy audio.

Verify actions from their actual results. Say a booking is confirmed only after the booking result succeeds, and avoid promising delivery merely because a message was accepted. For unavailable facts, use the configured knowledge fallback or offer a suitable handoff.
