Nixflex keeps things simple: there is no per-minute request limit on the API. The limits that matter are about **how many calls run at the same time** (concurrency), and the natural pacing of SMS and webhooks.

<Note>
  Most projects never come close to these limits. If you are planning a high-volume launch, tell us in advance at [support@nixflex.com](mailto:support@nixflex.com) and we will raise your concurrency before you need it.
</Note>

## Concurrent calls

Concurrency is the number of calls happening **at the same moment**, not per day.

By default your account can run **up to 10 concurrent calls**, and this can be raised to **50 on request**. If you need more than 50, we will size it with you.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Outbound calls queue when the limit is reached">
    If you trigger more outbound calls than your concurrency allows, the extra calls wait in a queue and dial automatically as slots free up. Nothing is dropped.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Inbound calls get a busy signal at the limit">
    Your limit is a single pool shared by inbound and outbound calls. If an inbound call arrives while you are at your limit, the caller gets a busy signal. The call is never answered, so **you are not billed for it** and it does not appear in your call history.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You can see when this happens">
    The **API Keys** page in the dashboard shows your peak concurrent calls this month, and warns you if any calls were turned away. If you see that warning, it is time to raise your limit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Raising your limit">
    Email [support@nixflex.com](mailto:support@nixflex.com) with the concurrency you need and we will raise it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Batch campaign concurrency

Within a batch campaign, calls are dialled in waves so you never launch everything at once. You can also throttle a campaign deliberately with `max_concurrent`, which is useful when you want to keep some headroom free for inbound calls:

```json
{
  "agent_id": "agent_xxx",
  "recipients": [ ... ],
  "max_concurrent": 5
}
```

A large campaign simply takes longer to work through at a lower `max_concurrent` - it never overloads your account.

## SMS pacing

SMS is paced by Twilio, not by Nixflex. The usual default is about **one message per second per sending number**. For higher volume:

- Add more sending numbers so messages spread across them.
- Ask Twilio to raise your per-number throughput.
- Use SMS campaigns, which respect Twilio's pacing automatically.

## Webhook delivery

Webhooks fire as fast as your endpoint can accept them, with no Nixflex-side throttle. To keep up:

- Return a `2xx` response quickly.
- Do the heavy work in a background queue, not inside the webhook handler.
